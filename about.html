About · 2018–2026

From technical writer to running two departments

I joined Presight (a G42 company) in 2018 as a Senior Technical Writer. Eight years later I run Documentation and Training inside a CXO-sponsored business unit, with a team of writers and trainers and a service catalog we sell to government and enterprise clients. The path between those two points is the actual case study.

  1. 1

    Stage 1

    Do the job well enough that the job grows

    Writing product documentation for enterprise AI and big data platforms revealed the real problem: the company didn’t have a documentation function, it had documents. So I started building the infrastructure, templates, standards, the SharePoint ecosystem, while still carrying a full writing load. Two years in, I was promoted to Manager; two years after that, to Senior Manager, Content Strategy.

  2. 2

    Stage 2

    Own the systems, then the people

    Standards only work if someone enforces and evolves them. Leading the documentation function meant owning the ecosystem that 200+ people adopted, reporting through Power BI, and hiring and developing the writers who worked in it. Training was added once it was clear the two should be built from one source.

  3. 3

    Stage 3

    Run it like an operation

    With both functions under one roof, the work shifted from producing content to running content and learning operations: capacity planning across writers and trainers, a training tracking system covering cohorts, attendance, assessments, and client feedback, monthly status and resource reporting to leadership, and the AI-assisted quality workflow that let a small team hold a consistent standard across a growing output. For a period this also included studio operations, which added production scheduling and vendor coordination to the mix.

  4. 4

    Stage 4

    A business, not a cost center

    In 2026 the company stood up the Applied Intelligence Academy to sell what my teams had been building internally. I chose to move into it, reporting to the CXO, because content and training were about to become revenue rather than overhead. My first job there was designing the service catalog: 30+ services, seven bundles, five engagement models.

What changed along the way

I stopped measuring success by documents shipped and started measuring it by adoption, audit results, and what clients bought.
